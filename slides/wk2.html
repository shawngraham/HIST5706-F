What is all this? Does it matter? How do you deal with that?

Imagine, if you will, that some aspect of your life became datafied. What would that look like? You would have to:
  • Identify your data: Who are you?
  • Describe your data: What are you interested in describing as a part of your dataset? What are you not?
  • Collect: Gather your data in one place.
  • Clean: Edit the dataset so that it’s in a presentable, consistent format. What tools do you use? Why? What do we lose when we clean data? Maybe there are alternative practices
  • Analyze: What stories are in your dataset?
  • Distribute: How would you share it back to me?
  • So let's give that a shot.

Omeka vs Mukurtu

(language pinched from gov't of canada, re fair)

  • Findable: Data should be easy to find by both humans and machines.
  • Accessible: Data should be available in a usable format with clear access protocols and constraints.
  • Interoperable: Data must be compatible across different systems and formats.
  • Reusable: Data should be structured and documented in such a way that others can easily and appropriately reuse it for further analysis or innovation.
    • From the Global Indigenous Data Alliance 
  • Collective Benefit Data ecosystems shall be designed and function in ways that enable Indigenous Peoples to derive benefit from the data
  • Authority to Control Indigenous Peoples’ rights and interests in Indigenous data must be recognised and their authority to control such data be empowered.
  • Responsibility Those working with Indigenous data have a responsibility to share how those data are used to support Indigenous Peoples’ self-
    determination and collective benefit
  • Ethics Indigenous Peoples’ rights and wellbeing should be the primary concern at all stages of the data life cycle and across the data ecosystem
